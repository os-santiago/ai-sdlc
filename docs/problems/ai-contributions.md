# Problem: Managing AI-generated contributions in open-source projects

## Context
As AI-assisted development tools become more prevalent, open-source projects face challenges in managing contributions generated by AI agents. These contributions may lack the same level of scrutiny, accountability, and licensing clarity as human-generated contributions. Projects need to establish clear policies to maintain legal cleanliness, protect against low-quality or malicious AI-generated PRs, and ensure design rigor.

## Options and Trade-offs

### Option 1: Require DCO sign-off for all commits, including AI-generated
- Pros:
  - Provides a clear audit trail for all contributions
  - Ensures contributors (including AI operators) acknowledge licensing obligations
  - Aligns with established open-source practices (e.g., Linux kernel)
- Cons:
  - AI agents cannot legally sign off, creating ambiguity
  - May discourage use of AI tools if process is seen as burdensome
  - Requires bot identity to be established for sign-off, which may not be legally recognized

### Option 2: Exempt AI-generated commits from DCO but require human review and vouch
- Pros:
  - Allows AI contributions while maintaining human oversight
  - Contributor vouch gate ensures maintainer endorsement for first-time AI-assisted PRs
  - Clear distinction between human and bot contributions in commit history
- Cons:
  - Creates two-tier system that may confuse contributors
  - Reliance on human review may not scale with high volume of AI contributions
  - Does not provide a formal licensing statement from the AI operator

### Option 3: Treat AI-generated commits as contributions from the AI operator (human or organization)
- Pros:
  - Simplifies process: AI operator signs off as they would for any contribution
  - Maintains single contribution standard
  - Legal clarity: the operator takes responsibility
- Cons:
  - Requires AI operators to be identified and agree to DCO
  - May be difficult to enforce in practice
  - Blurs line between tool and contributor

## Open questions
- How should we define and verify the identity of AI agents for contribution tracking?
- What level of human review is appropriate for AI-generated contributions (e.g., every PR, only first-time contributors)?
- Should we maintain a public list of approved AI agents/bots that are exempt from certain requirements?
- How do we handle contributions from AI tools where the operator is not the end-user (e.g., AI pair programming in a corporate environment)?
